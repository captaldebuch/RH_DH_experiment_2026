The Music of the Corpus: A Humanist's Frame

For digital humanists accustomed to uncovering latent patterns in vast text archives, the Riemann Zeta Function serves as the ultimate macro-analytic algorithm—a mathematical distant reading lens that transforms the infinite dataset of natural numbers into an underlying network of irreducible building blocks: the prime numbers. While the additive series of the zeta function acts like a weighted corpus filter and its Euler product exposes the primary sources driving the system, its non-trivial zeros are the fundamental harmonic frequencies—the critical "silences" or wave interference points—that dictate precisely how primes are distributed.

These zeros matter immensely because, through Riemann's Explicit Formula, they act as the exact acoustic wave correction terms that convert broad statistical estimates into the true, pinpoint locations of prime numbers; if any zero were to stray off the critical line (Re(s) = 1/2), it would introduce rogue frequencies, causing wild structural fluctuations in prime density that could dismantle modern cryptography. The Riemann Hypothesis posits that all non-trivial zeros align with perfect symmetry along this single axis of equilibrium, asserting that the universe of numbers is governed not by random chaos, but by a universal law of optimal balance and minimal noise.

In this work, we apply the digital humanities toolkit—corpus analysis, semantic knowledge graphs, multi-agent interpretation, and machine certification—to render 167+ years of RH literature into a transparent, formally audited mathematical architecture. The result is not a proof, but a rigorous clarification of what remains unknown and why.

How This Experiment Works

The corpus is treated as a shared laboratory, not a passive bibliography. Four connected operations:

  1. Distant reading: Collect and normalize papers, authors, dates, themes at corpus scale into knowledge graph (21,942 nodes, 15,338 relations)
  2. Model dialogue: Ask Claude Sonnet 5, GPT-5.6 Sol, Gemini 3.1, Mistral Medium 3.5, DeepSeek-v4, and Kimi K3 to propose interpretations, links, counterexamples, formalization targets. Disagreement retained as research record.
  3. Close reading and audit: Return to source passages, check hypotheses and scope, label candidate theorems without treating extraction as validation
  4. Autoformalization: Translate selected statements into Lean interfaces and proofs, making dependencies explicit in Formalization Suite

Methodological boundary: An LLM suggestion is a research prompt, not a theorem. A corpus link is not evidence of implication. A Lean declaration is not automatically a proof of RH. The knowledge graph keeps these layers connected but visibly distinct.

The Dialectic of Error Correction: Exposing Premature Claims & Remediation

The necessity of this adversarial parliament is proven by the real history of errors uncovered and corrected during the project. In earlier exploratory phases, GPT-5.6 Sol generated proofs that appeared syntactically complete, leading to a premature internal belief that the entire RH reduction was solved.

Rigorous adversarial auditing (archived in docs/) uncovered critical flaws: mathematically false axioms in proofs/route-c/ (such as mobius_partial_sum_bounded $|M(x)| \le 2$, weil_bound_mobius, and tautological ZetaZero definitions), regulator conflations in Session 4 ($\rho^m \sigma^\ell$ vs. $\tau^{m\ell}$), and omitted singular modes in early Hurwitz equations. The parliament exposed these flaws, quarantined the legacy prototypes, and systematically rebuilt the entire active hierarchy in proofs/NBMellinTools/ with 0 custom axioms.

Five-Layer Verification: (1) Kernel verification via lake build (0 custom axioms), (2) Close reading against peer-reviewed sources, (3) Axiom audit trails, (4) Documentation chains linking sessions to literature, and (5) Intellectual honesty statements explicitly naming the open frontier problem ($H_{15}$).

Vocabulary & Concepts

Key mathematical and digital-humanities terms used throughout the project, with explicit connections to the work.

Analytic Continuation

Extending a function from one region (e.g., Re(s)>1) to a larger region (complex plane) while preserving properties.

Project: Sessions 6–8 use AC to relate D(s,a/q) on Re(s)=3/2 to behavior at Re(s)=1/2.

Bettin-Connes Framework

Modern approach to analytic number theory using explicit integral representations of ζ and derivatives.

Project: Core methodology for Sessions 6–9; rational decompositions locked to their papers.

Critical Line

The line in the complex plane where Re(s)=1/2. RH asserts all non-trivial zeros of ζ lie here.

Project: Sessions 8–9 contour shift moves integrals toward Re(s)=1/2 to isolate H15 decay.

Dirichlet Series

Infinite series ∑ aₙ/nˢ. Riemann ζ is the special case where aₙ=1.

Project: Estermann E(s)=∑d(n)/nˢ (divisor coefficients) is the core Dirichlet series in Session 8.

Distant Reading

Digital humanities technique to analyze large text corpora computationally (text mining, NLP, network analysis).

Project: Extracted themes, citations, and patterns from 498 papers into 21,942-node knowledge graph.

Formal Verification

Proving mathematical statements are logically correct using a proof assistant. Every step machine-checked.

Project: Lean 4 kernel verifies 8,518 jobs with 0 custom axioms. Logically sound at highest rigor.

Functional Equation (Zeta)

ζ(s) = 2ˢπˢ⁻¹sin(πs/2)Γ(1−s)ζ(1−s). Connects ζ(s) to ζ(1−s).

Project: Session 8 applies this to pole-free Hurwitz series; enables contour shift and H15 extraction.

Kernel (Lean)

Core type-checking and proof-verification engine of Lean 4. Relatively small, well-audited.

Project: Every theorem kernel-checked. Provides machine-readable assurance of logical soundness.

Lean 4

Modern proof assistant and functional programming language designed for formal mathematics.

Project: All active reduction proofs formalized in Lean; 36 active imported modules with Mathlib.

Lemma

Auxiliary theorem, often stepping stone to larger theorem.

Project: ~150+ lemmas formalized from classical papers (Hurwitz bounds, explicit formulas, etc.).

Mellin Transform

Integral transform relating function on positive reals to analytic properties in complex plane.

Project: Session 5–8 use Mellin inversion to extract decay bounds from ζ-function analysis.

Nyman-Beurling Criterion

RH is equivalent to completeness of a certain space of functions (fractional parts of rationals).

Project: Session 1–2 entry point; reduces RH to L² harmonic weight analysis formalized in Lean.

Riemann Hypothesis

All non-trivial zeros of ζ(s) lie on critical line Re(s)=1/2. Posed by Riemann in 1859.

Project: Central object; proved equivalent to H15CenteredAggregateEstimate (frontier problem).
